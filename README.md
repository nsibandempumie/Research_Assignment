### My Research Process

**Step 1 — Online Research First**
I researched terms independently using:
- **Investopedia** — for clear, reliable definitions of financial and business terms (CAC, ROI, CLV, Churn Rate, etc.)
- **YouTube explainers** — particularly useful for visual concepts like the Sales Funnel, A/B Testing, and the four types of analytics

**Step 2 — Using Claude (AI) as a Writing and Structuring Tool**
After researching independently, I used Claude (by Anthropic) in a specific and deliberate way — not to generate definitions for me, but to help structure and articulate my own understanding. My process per term:
1. Researched the term using Investopedia or YouTube
2. Connected it to my real-world experience as a phlebotomist in a medical laboratory
3. Explained my understanding to Claude, using medical laboratory examples
4. Asked Claude to restructure and write it up properly based on what I had shared

> **On AI transparency:** The BrightLearn assignment brief explicitly states that students may use AI tools to *understand* concepts, but all submitted definitions must be in the student's own words. My definitions are grounded in my own professional experience and understanding — Claude helped give that understanding a clear, structured voice.

**Step 3 — Grounding Every Term in My Professional Experience**
I work as a phlebotomist in a medical laboratory, which gave me a practical lens for almost every concept. Examples:
- **KPIs** exist in a lab as turnaround time (TAT) targets and sample rejection rates
- **Churn Rate** translates to patients or referring doctors who stop using a laboratory's services
- **Segmentation** applies when labs categorise patients by test type, urgency, or referring facility
- **Descriptive Analytics** is what a lab does every time it produces a monthly report of total samples processed

### Key Things I Learned

The insight that stuck with me most was how the four types of analytics build on each other in a logical progression:

```
Descriptive Analytics   →   "What happened?"
        ↓
Diagnostic Analytics    →   "Why did it happen?"
        ↓
Predictive Analytics    →   "What is likely to happen next?"
        ↓
Prescriptive Analytics  →   "What should we do about it?"
```

Each level depends on the one before it. You can't diagnose a problem without first describing what happened. You can't prescribe an action without a prediction to act on.

Other concepts that clicked in a meaningful way:
- **Metric vs. KPI** — every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI. A number only becomes a KPI when it is directly tied to a strategic goal that matters to the business.
- **CAC vs. CLV and the CLV:CAC ratio** — a business can be growing fast and still be destroying value if it costs more to acquire a customer than that customer will ever generate.
- **Marketing Attribution** — understanding that a customer might touch 5 different channels before buying, and that deciding which channel "gets the credit" is a complex analytical problem, not a simple one.
